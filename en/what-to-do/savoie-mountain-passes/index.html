The great passes of Savoie: altitudes, openings, access

Iseran, Galibier, Madeleine, Croix de Fer, Mont-Cenis: the road passes of Savoie, with what the sources actually say about their altitude and opening date — contradictions included.

How this selection is made — Compiled from our verified fact sheets: this page's contents are exactly the output of the stated selector, never a hand-written list. It updates whenever a sheet changes.

The selection · 15

Col de l'Iseran

Bonneval-sur-Arc
FreeParkingWinter ✓

Open right across since 12 June 2026. Free access on the D902 between Val-d'Isère and Bonneval-sur-Arc. Free parking. The only road crossing inside the Parc national de la Vanoise.

See details →

Col du Petit-Saint-Bernard

Séez
FreeWinter ✓

2,188 m, the France–Italy border above Séez. Opened on the Italian side 22 May 2026, on the French side 24 May. The Chanousia garden opens only in July, €3 — not when the road opens.

See details →

Cormet de Roselend

Beaufort
FreeParkingWinter ✓

Pass at 1,968 m between the Beaufortain and the Tarentaise, on the D 925. No restaurant, no toilets at the top. The 2026 reopening was reported at two different times.

See details →

Col de la Croix de Fer

Saint-Sorlin-d'Arves
FreeParkingWinter ✓

Between the Maurienne and the Oisans on the RD926 and the D526. Opening announced for 20 May 2026 under alternating traffic. Free parking, picnic area, start of the three-lakes walk.

See details →

Col de la Madeleine

Saint-François-Longchamp
FreeParkingWinter ✓

Open since 29 May 2026. Between La Chambre in the Maurienne and La Léchère in the Tarentaise, on the RD 213. Free access. 1,993 m per IGN, 2,000 m on the stele.

See details →

Col du Galibier

Valloire
FreeParkingWinter ✓

Summit road at 2,642 m, tunnel at 2,556 m: two separate openings in 2026, on 29 May and 5 June. Free access from Valloire. Cyclists are banned from the tunnel.

See details →

Col de la Loze

Courchevel
FreeWinter ✓

Pass at 2,304 m between Courchevel and Méribel, reserved for bicycles: cars banned. 7 km on the Méribel side, 6.5 on the Courchevel side, a 23% wall.

See details →

Col des Saisies

Hauteluce
FreeWinter ✓

Pass at 1,650 m between the Beaufortain and the Val d'Arly, commune of Hauteluce. Open all year: an inhabited resort, not a high pass that shuts for winter.

See details →

Col du Glandon

Saint-Colomban-des-Villards
FreeParkingWinter ✓

Pass at 1,924 m between the Isère and the Savoie, 2.5 km from the Croix de Fer. In 2026, access from Saint-Colomban-des-Villards did not open before 19 June.

See details →

Col du Mont-Cenis

Val-Cenis
FreeParkingWinter ✓

Upper Maurienne pass above a hydroelectric reservoir. Three published altitudes (2,081, 2,083, 2,085 m). Entirely in France since 1947, despite what you will read.

See details →

Col du Télégraphe

Valloire
FreeParkingWinter ✓

Pass at 1,566 m between Saint-Michel-de-Maurienne and Valloire, open all year. The fort, ten minutes' walk away, opens in summer only, Wednesdays and Thursdays.

See details →

Col du Chaussy

Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne
FreeParking

Pass at 1,533 m above the Maurienne, famous for the Lacets de Montvernier: 17 or 18 hairpins depending on the source. 14 km at 7.5% to the top.

See details →

Col du Mollard

Albiez-Montrond
Free

Pass at 1,638 m above Albiez-Montrond, known for its 45 hairpins. A lake and a fountain at the top; no supervised swimming is documented.

See details →

Cormet d'Arêches

Beaufort
Free

Beaufortain pass at 2,107 m. The tarmac stops at Pichu: the last 3.2 km are a track at 8-9%. Not to be confused with the Cormet de Roselend.

See details →

Signal de Bisanne

Hauteluce
Free

Summit at 1,930 m above Les Saisies. The 2016 Tour de France stopped at 1,723 m, at the resort, not the Signal. Two sides: 14.35 km or 4.35 km.

See details →
