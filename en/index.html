Bonneval-sur-Arc
Col de l'Iseran

The Col de l'Iseran links Val-d'Isère, in the Tarentaise, to Bonneval-sur-Arc, in the Maurienne. It is the only road crossing inside the Parc national de la Vanoise. Its altitude is given as 2,770 m or 2,764 m depending on the page — including within one official site.

Free
Saint-Sorlin-d'Arves
Col de la Croix de Fer

The Col de la Croix de Fer links the Maurienne, at Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne, to the Oisans, in the Isère. It must not be confused on any point with the Col du Glandon, three kilometres to the west, which shares its road until the last two hundred metres.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Courchevel
Col de la Loze

The Col de la Loze links Courchevel to Méribel at 2,304 m on a road closed to cars. Seven kilometres on the Méribel side, 6.5 on the Courchevel side, and a 23% wall.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Saint-François-Longchamp
Col de la Madeleine

The Col de la Madeleine links the Maurienne, at La Chambre, to the Tarentaise, at La Léchère, by way of Saint-François-Longchamp. Its altitude is 1,993 m per IGN, but the road signs and the stele read 2,000 m and the old signage read 1,984 m.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Hauteluce
Col des Saisies

The Col des Saisies links the Beaufortain to the Val d'Arly, in the commune of Hauteluce. Inhabited and open all year, it does not close like the high passes.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne
Col du Chaussy

The Col du Chaussy tops out at 1,533 m, but its fame comes from the Lacets de Montvernier below. Seventeen or eighteen hairpins, depending which source you read.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Valloire
Col du Galibier

The Col du Galibier links Valloire, in Savoie, to the Col du Lautaret, in the Hautes-Alpes. Two roads, two altitudes, two opening dates: the tunnel at 2,556 m reopened on 29 May 2026, the summit road at 2,642 m on 5 June.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Saint-Colomban-des-Villards
Col du Glandon

The Col du Glandon tops out at 1,924 m between the Isère and the Savoie, 2.5 km from the Croix de Fer. In 2026 its Savoyard side did not open before 19 June.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Albiez-Montrond
Col du Mollard

The Col du Mollard opens onto the hamlet of the same name above Albiez-Montrond, below the Aiguilles d'Arves. Forty-five hairpins, on a source nobody corroborates.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Val-Cenis
Col du Mont-Cenis

The Col du Mont-Cenis opens the upper Maurienne towards Italy above a reservoir. It is not a border crossing: since 1947 it has lain entirely in France.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Séez
Col du Petit-Saint-Bernard

The Col du Petit-Saint-Bernard, at 2,188 m, links Séez, in Savoie, to La Thuile, in the Valle d'Aosta. Three different calendars overlap here: the road's, the Chanousia botanical garden's and the Hospice's. Do not conflate them.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Valloire
Col du Télégraphe

The Col du Télégraphe crosses at 1,566 m between Saint-Michel-de-Maurienne and Valloire. Antechamber to the Galibier — and unlike it, open all year.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Beaufort
Cormet d'Arêches

The Cormet d'Arêches crosses the Beaufortain at 2,107 m. The tarmac stops at Pichu: the last 3.2 km are a track at 8-9%.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Beaufort
Cormet de Roselend

The Cormet de Roselend crosses the Beaufortain–Tarentaise divide at 1,968 m. A car park, a view, and nothing else: no restaurant, no toilets.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Hauteluce
Signal de Bisanne

The Signal de Bisanne looks over Les Saisies from 1,930 m. The Tour de France climbed it in 2016 — but stopped 207 m lower, at the resort.

Saint-Pierre-d'Albigny
Château de Miolans

A listed fortress on its rock spur above the Combe de Savoie: 12th-century keep, the dukes' state prison — the Marquis de Sade was locked up here — and a panorama from Mont Blanc to the Vercors.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Le Bourget-du-Lac
Château de Thomas II

A residence of the counts of Savoy begun in 1248 on the shore of the Lac du Bourget, ruined by fire, listed in 1983. Free courtyard year-round, museum tower in summer.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Paid
Chambéry
Château des ducs de Savoie

Seat of the counts then dukes of Savoy, now home to the préfecture and the Département. The interiors — including the 15th-century Sainte-Chapelle — open on guided tours only.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Mercury
Fort de Tamié

Built in 1876 by 600 Italian workers to command the Albertville square, the fort is free to visit: the Tour du Fort trail, a picnic area and three belvederes.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Aussois
Fort Victor-Emmanuel

The most imposing of the Esseillon's five Sardinian forts, built for 1,500 men. Free wandering all year — though the access road closes with the snow.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Paid
Avrieux
Redoute Marie-Thérèse

One of the five forts of the Esseillon barrier, built by the Kingdom of Sardinia in the early 19th century — now an interpretation centre, wheelchair-accessible without assistance.

Directions ↗Official site ↗
Free
Chignin
Tours de Chignin

Seven medieval towers — five still visible, says the Parc des Bauges — rise from the vines of Chignin. No ticket office: a very easy 3.3 km trail passes at their feet.
